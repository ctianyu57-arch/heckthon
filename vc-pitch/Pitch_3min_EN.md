> **394 words. At 135 wpm that is 2:55 — with the demo pause, about 3:00.**

> **Do not add a single sentence.** If you are over time, use Appendix A rather than speeding up.

> **Bold = must say.** Everything else can be dropped without losing the story.

**Would you be nervous?"**

## [0:18 – 0:44] The problem · Slides 3 → 4

> Skip slide 3 fast. Stop on 4.

"Business school teaches you the knowledge of fundraising. Valuation. Term sheets. Business plans.

**All of it can be memorised.**

**But the moment that decides the outcome isn't knowledge. It's response.**

The follow-up you didn't expect. The number you can't recall. The second her tone goes cold.

**No model answer. No second attempt. And nobody will practise it with you a hundred times."

"So I built this investor. Sarah Chen, partner at a seed fund.

**She carries a ledger you never see.** Interest. Trust. Three red lines she never states.

**Say the same sentence at thirty and at eighty, and she responds completely differently.**

Low score, she wraps up politely. High score, she offers you resources.

**So a memorised script doesn't survive."**

# Appendix B · The four questions you will probably get

**"Isn't this just a chatbot?"**
> No. A chatbot answers what you ask. **The whole point here is that she carries a ledger you cannot see, and her posture changes because of how you performed.** That graded reaction is the product.

**"Does the AI actually sound like a real VC?"**
> Honestly — **it behaves like a competent senior VC. It is not a person.** No network details, no real portfolio memory. But the goal was never to replace a human. It gets a student through the eighty percent that is learnable, and leaves the twenty percent of surprise for the real room.

**"Do students actually learn anything?"**
> Yes, because the debrief doesn't give a score. **It lays out what every line of hers actually meant** — *"she said she was still getting smart on the space; that was an excuse, if she believed in you she'd learn it herself."* **A real VC will never show you that.**

**"Would these two features work without AI?"**
> No. One has to read free text and infer what that situation implies. The other has to judge a spoken answer that was never written down. **You cannot write rules for either.**

# Appendix D · Delivery notes for a non-native speaker

Three things that give you away faster than grammar:

1. **Rushing.** Nervous English speakers speed up. Force a half-second pause after each bolded beat.
2. **Filling silence with "um".** Your own filler will be scored by the very product you are pitching. Let silence sit.
3. **Reading the slide.** Memorise only the opening line of each section. The rest can follow.

**Pronounce these correctly before you go up:**
- *Sarah Chen* — SAIR-uh CHEN
- *pro-rata* — pro-RAH-tuh
- *SAFE* — say it as a word, "sayf", not spelled out
- *valuation cap* — CAP, not "cape"
