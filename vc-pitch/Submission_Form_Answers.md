## 5. Brief description of your solution

> **主版本（约 190 词，适合不限字数的输入框）**

```
We built a VC simulation environment where business school students practise the first
fundraising conversation before they ever meet a real investor.

The student plays the founder and talks to an AI venture capitalist who carries a hidden
ledger: her interest, her trust, and three red lines she never states. Throughout the
conversation the student cannot see her real score - only her tone, the depth of her
questions, and how long her answers are. Her posture shifts across six tiers, so the same
sentence gets a completely different response depending on how the student has performed
so far. Memorised scripts do not survive.

The investor also reads who the founder is before she speaks. The student describes himself
in a few lines of free text - a solo nurse with eight years on a ward, a second-time
founder, an engineer who cannot sell - and the model infers what that situation actually
implies: what it buys him, what she will suspect, and what she will press him on. A
solo non-technical founder opens to "You are on your own, then. Who is actually building
this?"; a technical founder opens to "You can build it. Who is going to sell it?" Same
product, visibly different interview.

Fundraising is spoken, so the student answers out loud. Speech is transcribed, but the
product does not stop at the words: it measures delivery - pace, stalls, filler words,
duration - and the investor reacts to how it sounded, not just what was said. The same
stuttering answer scores eleven points better typed than spoken, because typing deletes the
doubt that a listener hears.

Real investors never give the real reason for passing; they use presentable excuses. When
a round ends, the product reveals both columns side by side - what she said, and what she
actually thought. It then shows the stronger answer the student could have given, and
closes with three specific things to practise before the next meeting.

The outcome: students become fluent in the language and logic of venture capital before
they are ever in the room. A real VC gives you one attempt. This gives you a hundred.
```
