# Appendix C · The four questions you will get

**"Isn't this just a chatbot?"**
> No. A chatbot answers what you ask. **She carries a ledger you cannot see, and her posture changes because of what you did.** That graded reaction is the product — and you just watched her face change when he broke a red line.

**"Does it sound like a real VC?"**
> Honestly — **it behaves like a competent senior VC. It is not a person.** No network details, no portfolio memory. But it was never meant to replace a human. It gets a student through the eighty percent that is learnable.

**"How do you prove students improve?"**
> **The trend line in the debrief.** Every session stores interest, trust and overall. Six sessions on one line is evidence. A single score proves nothing.

**"What if the network dies during your demo?"**
> **That is why I pre-loaded a demo build.** It runs a full negotiation, a red line, the sound, and the debrief with zero network calls. Underneath, there is also an offline rule engine that keeps the product playable with no API key at all. **The design decision was: never let the environment decide whether the demo works.**

# Appendix E · Delivery notes for a non-native speaker

Three things that give you away faster than grammar:

1. **Rushing.** Nervous English speeds up. Force half a second of silence after each bolded beat.
2. **Filling silence with "um".** *Your own filler will be scored by the product you are selling.*
3. **Reading the slide.** Memorise only the first line of each section.

**Pronounce correctly:**
- *Sarah Chen* — SAIR-uh CHEN
- *pro-rata* — pro-RAH-tuh
- *SAFE* — "sayf", as a word
- *valuation cap* — CAP, not "cape"
- *conviction* — kun-VIK-shun (not "con-VIC-tion")
